# Settlement Compass Experiment 002 — 10 vs. 20 Training DAGs

**Date:** August 13, 2026  
**Author:** Brian Tenneson

## Question

Holding the benchmark targets fixed, does adding ten more randomly selected true proof DAGs from the same pre-Infinity region of `set.mm` improve the learned settlement compass?

The base theory remains the deliberately weakened ZF-style theory with the Axiom of Infinity withheld. The frozen test set remains the same 50 opaque targets from Benchmark 001: 49 theorem/corollary-style targets plus one hidden copy of the Axiom of Infinity statement, whose audit class is `I`.

This experiment is a **retrospective proof-DAG navigation diagnostic**, not yet a full P/R/I/C ATP settlement race. The 49 targets with known proof DAGs can be scored by proof-region and distance-to-settlement metrics. The hidden Infinity target remains in the frozen 50 but cannot be assigned a proof-distance score because it has no proof DAG in the weakened theory; it must ultimately be handled by the independence agent with a valid independence certificate.

## Training sets

The 10-DAG condition uses the original training roots:

`pwidg, axreplem, f1ores, fiinf2g, eqop, frpomin, rabab, rabun2, eusn, elun2`.

The 20-DAG condition keeps those ten and adds ten reproducibly drawn eligible true proof DAGs:

`pm4.72, resindi, simp112, anandi3, nlim1, equs5, diffi, dfpss2, axextg, sbal1`.

The additional ten were excluded if they were among the 49 ordinary test theorems or if their transitive proof-dependency closure contained a test theorem, reducing direct target leakage.

## Results

| Metric | 10 DAGs | 20 DAGs | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mean proof-region AUC | 0.7873 | 0.8118 | +0.0244 |
| Mean Spearman correlation with true proof distance | 0.2742 | 0.3198 | +0.0456 |
| Mean absolute error of distance estimate | 3.3938 | 2.6827 | -0.7110 |
| Median rank of first proof-DAG node | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| Median rank of first direct parent / immediate tunnel | 547 | 298 | **-249** |
| Mean precision among top 10 candidates | 0.6224 | 0.5184 | -0.1041 |
| Targets where compass beat random on direct-parent rank | 25/49 | 28/49 | +3 |
| Targets where random beat compass on direct-parent rank | 24/49 | 21/49 | -3 |

## Interpretation

The answer is **yes, with one important mixed signal**. Adding ten more proof DAGs improved several of the metrics most closely tied to the compass hypothesis. It became better at distinguishing the correct proof region from distractors, its predicted distance to settlement correlated more strongly with the true graph distance, and its mean distance error fell substantially. Most strikingly, the median rank of the first immediate proof parent improved from 547 to 298. On the same frozen targets, the 20-DAG compass beat the random ordering on immediate-tunnel rank in 28 cases and lost in 21, compared with 25 wins and 24 losses for the 10-DAG compass.

The result is not uniformly positive. Mean precision at 10 decreased from 0.6224 to 0.5184. Thus more training data improved **global proof-ocean localization and estimated descent toward settlement**, while the very top of the ranked candidate list became less concentrated with proof-DAG nodes. This suggests that proof-region relevance and immediate-next-tunnel selection are related but distinct learning problems.

A conservative summary is:

> **Twenty training DAGs learned the proof-ocean geometry better than ten on most distance/navigation measures, but not on every ranking measure.**

This is evidence for continued study, not yet evidence that the compass accelerates an actual ATP under equal search budgets.

## Infinity target

The hidden Infinity target remains part of the unchanged 50-target benchmark. It was not used in the 49-case proof-DAG metric calculation because no ordinary proof DAG exists for it in ZF without Infinity. It must not be scored as independent merely because proof and refutation searches fail. A future full AMLD run must send all 50 opaque WFFs to P, R, I, and C and require a valid independence certificate for the special target.

## Next experiment

The next scientifically stronger experiment should retain **actual search DAGs**, including failed branches, and compare compass-on against compass-off under identical candidate generation, seeds, axioms, verifier, and resource budgets. That will test whether the improved distance model produces an actual reduction in expansions or wall-clock time to certified settlement.
